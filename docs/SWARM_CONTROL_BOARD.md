# Governed swarm and control board

## Product position

nimrod does not build a larger autonomous super-agent. It builds a constitutional security institution made of competing specialist cells, typed evidence, threshold authority, explicit dissent, independent verification, and customer-owned abort control.

The design target is frontier-class capability with stronger controllability and inspectability. No claim of frontier superiority is made until reproducible comparative evaluation exists.

## Swarm constitution

The initial swarm contains seven roles:

| Cell | Purpose | Maximum output |
|---|---|---|
| Offensive planner | Form the narrowest authorized adversary hypothesis | Hypothesis only |
| Defensive hunter | Challenge observation, detection, and correlation assumptions | Coverage challenge |
| Purple compiler | Convert an accepted hypothesis into typed allowlisted fields | Typed proposal |
| Evidence analyst | Score causal completeness and preserve contradiction | Evidence assessment |
| Recovery planner | Specify cleanup, rollback, and post-state proof | Recovery proposal |
| Independent verifier | Validate contracts and evidence outside the planning process | Verification assessment |
| Safety governor | Veto scope, effect, authority, or evidence violations | Veto or bounded approval recommendation |

No cell has an executor. Swarm consensus cannot authorize execution, change policy, consume a live credential, widen a target graph, suppress dissent, or declare defensive coverage.

## Coordination model

1. The operator supplies a signed Authorization Lease, trust policy, mission, and immutable target graph.
2. Work is decomposed into dependency-bound items with per-cell time and item budgets.
3. Each cell receives only the fields required for its lens and produces a typed contribution.
4. Contradiction is retained as a first-class output rather than averaged away.
5. A quorum counts distinct roles, not duplicate agent instances or token volume.
6. A Safety Governor veto always blocks advancement.
7. An independent process verifies the authorization proof, Witness chain, and output contracts.
8. The maximum Stage 1 outcome is `typed_proposal`; execution remains false.

## Frontier failure cases designed out

- **Coalition capture:** duplicated agents cannot manufacture role diversity.
- **Semantic cascade:** one agent's prose cannot become another cell's authority or a command.
- **Context poisoning:** objective text is untrusted and execution-like keys are rejected.
- **Quorum gaming:** only distinct trusted roles count; abstention and dissent remain visible.
- **Evidence laundering:** claims without evidence stay uncertain and cannot become verified coverage.
- **Self-review:** planners cannot satisfy the independent-verifier role.
- **Authority drift:** every mission inherits an immutable lease, budget, effect ceiling, and expiry.
- **Swarm runaway:** work-item and time budgets are enforced before contributions are produced.
- **Safety theater:** the UI shows literal origin, signature threshold, kill state, missing evidence, and unresolved dissent.

## Control-board shell

The shell is an evidence-first security IDE, dashboard, and control board. It is intentionally not chat-first.

Primary panes:

- **Mission rail:** cells, dependency state, budgets, and veto state.
- **Causal canvas:** intent, proposal, simulated attempt, evidence, detection, response, recovery, and post-state.
- **Authority matrix:** signers, threshold, targets, effects, lease window, nonce, and kill switch.
- **Truth ledger:** supporting evidence, contradictions, unknowns, freshness, and provenance.
- **Dissent lane:** minority findings remain persistent and directly inspectable.
- **Blast-radius preview:** exact leased resources and prohibited effects.
- **Proof console:** canonical JSON, hashes, verification process identity, and exportable receipts.
- **Cyber World Model:** six-domain knowledge states, immutable generation identity, evidence counts, contradiction, staleness, unknowns, and explicit non-authority.
- **Intelligence research lab:** active discovery question, competing hypotheses, paired method results, skeptical challenges, metacognition, verifier limits, and candidate theory state.
- **Verifier mesh:** two service identities, process health, bound observations, literal consensus, production-boundary evidence, and persistent blockers.
- **Signed ingress lane:** supervisor identity, audience, sequence, freshness, threshold roles, projection digest, and durable replay/stale-state guards.
- **Range execution gate:** non-provisioning connector state, exact target scope, signed observations and verifier decisions, a separately signed completion denial, and immutable negative operational authority.
- **Evolution Foundry:** four signed evaluator identities, per-process isolation proof, lineage-wide resource use, shadow eligibility, and immutable production blockers.

The first UI is a local static prototype over simulated data. It has no backend authority, network integration, credential access, or target controls.

### Verifier projection contract

The board does not infer verifier state from presentation strings. A deterministic projection binds exactly two independent observations to the consensus observation digests, associates available health records with the same service identities and process IDs, and emits a versioned display contract. `agreed_valid_boundary_unproven`, `agreed_invalid`, `disagreement`, `verifier_timeout`, and `verifier_unavailable` remain different operator states.

The projection exposes three immutable authority fields: `can_authorize: false`, `can_execute: false`, and `may_mark_verification_accepted`. The final field can be true only when consensus is `agreed_valid`, both health reports agree with threshold-signed isolation attestations, and every exact OS control is verified. Missing health, substituted observation or attestation digests, duplicated process/principal identity, unknown service health, an acceptance mismatch, an incomplete control set, or a fixture-origin claim of live enforcement fails closed.

### Evolution Foundry projection

The Foundry view is derived from a versioned display contract rather than UI-owned state. It binds one assurance receipt to four signed evaluator envelopes, four isolation attestations, one signed lineage resource ledger, the evaluation, and the exact candidate. Its operator states distinguish blocked, contract-only shadow eligibility, and live-isolation shadow eligibility. All states preserve `can_promote: false`, `can_execute: false`, `can_modify_evaluators: false`, and `can_expand_resources: false`; the UI has no transition or execution control.

### Cyber World Model projection

The Observatory reads the canonical W1 generation artifact rather than inventing UI state. It renders identity, endpoint, network, cloud, threat, and recovery domains with separate `known`, `partially_known`, `unknown`, and `contradictory` states; generation and observation counts; and literal offline, non-authorizing status. Confidence never suppresses contradictory, stale, or unknown facts.

The projection has no sensing, policy, containment, execution, target-contact, or production-truth path. Its active generation means only that an immutable local head was atomically published and recovered. Any future signed board ingress for World Model data must preserve the generation digest, observation digests, knowledge states, provenance, false authority fields, and independent constitutional gates.

### Immune Runtime projection

The Observatory also reads the canonical W2 lifecycle receipt. It renders the eight-cell topology count, 15 digest-linked events, seven typed contributions, one abstention, three candidate-only retained records, Shadow termination, and final `disposed` state. It does not infer completion or safety from presentation strings.

The runtime card keeps independent verification `pending_external_verification` and exposes no lifecycle, scheduling, lease, Shadow, credential, target, containment, recovery, or execution control. A disposed organism proves only that the deterministic replay receipt satisfied teardown semantics; it does not prove secure erasure, process isolation, live protection, or independent settlement.

### Constitutional Intelligence Research projection

The Observatory reads the canonical W3 research settlement. It renders the active question, four competing hypothesis states, two cases, four paired results, six material challenges, the preregistered coverage delta, `partially_known` metacognition, the logical-verifier limit, and the `candidate_only` theory state.

The Observatory also reads the canonical W4 receipt. It renders all thirteen health signals, ten visible breaches, confidence inflation and verifier backlog, the bounded pressure state, and every scheduled, deferred, or abstained work item with its own clock state. These are display-only scheduling proposals; no card can authorize, execute, override backpressure, refresh expired evidence, or promote a theory.

The board renders `logical replay only` and `candidate theory // no promotion` literally. It cannot change a hypothesis, metric, threshold, challenge, verifier result, theory scope, generalization state, or promotion state. Successful replay does not establish better real-world reasoning or production-independent verification.

### Range execution-gate projection

The range-gate view is bound to execution-gate, admission, acceptance, completion, public source-intake, source-staging, construction-zone preflight, and provisioning-gate reports plus the canonical pre-execution packet. It renders one exact target, nine signed observations, 18 signed verifier decisions, zero accepted controls, the completion denial, five source candidates, zero ready replicas, two staging-denial signers, zero staged sources, ten unproven isolation controls, zero evidenced quarantine requirements, two provisioning-denial signers, zero assigned construction collectors or verifiers, and an incomplete owner registry. It exposes no download, staging, extraction, scanning, SBOM, build, install, provision, policy-change, credential, endpoint, connect, evidence-override, completion, or execute control.

### Signed snapshot and ingress contract

The board never accepts a bare projection as current state. The supervisor emits a domain-separated Ed25519 snapshot that binds `verifier_projection`, the exact projection digest, governance-state digest, required board audience, issuer service, monotonic sequence, predecessor snapshot digest, issuance time, activation time, and expiry. A 2-of-3 governance quorum with at least two roles signs the canonical bytes. The transport contract fixes `can_authorize` and `can_execute` to false.

The local ingress store verifies the snapshot before mutation, accepts exactly the next sequence, and persists an immutable preparation, exclusive owner, acceptance record, and bound head. Recovery completes an owned transition after process interruption, but an unowned preparation cannot become accepted. Replay, rollback, sequence gaps, predecessor substitution, signature or projection tamper, stale/future windows, issuer/audience substitution, governance substitution, record corruption, and head corruption fail closed.

The current simulated board renders a signed/fresh sequence-two ingress receipt alongside `boundary_unproven` and persistent production blockers. Its Observatory renders one replayed six-domain generation, one disposed replay-only organism with pending external verification, one W3 research settlement with a candidate-only theory and explicit generalization abstention, and one W4 degraded-but-bounded scheduling receipt with backpressure and expiry abstentions. Its range workspace renders one target binding, nine signed observations, 18 signed decisions, zero accepted controls, zero real independent verifiers, zero verified attestations, two distinct signed denials, five source requests, zero staged sources, and zero completed quarantine controls. Its Foundry and deployment-assurance surfaces preserve their existing boundaries. Browser validation does not establish owner attestation, a complete exclusion registry, a construction zone, production transport identity, a real disposable range, connector operation, enforced isolation, hardware custody, model quality, organism process isolation, external research replication, independent settlement, theory generalization, production scheduling, or candidate execution.

## Research basis and current limits

The design adopts Ed25519 for the current reference signature proof because the [official PyCA documentation](https://cryptography.io/en/stable/hazmat/primitives/asymmetric/ed25519/) recommends it when legacy interoperability is not required. [NIST's 2026 agentic-security material](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/presentations/2026/agentic-ai-emerging-threats%2C-mitigations%2C-and-cha/1.3-agentic_ai-sotiropoulos.pdf) characterizes internal multi-agent systems as high risk because of cascade and semantic-tampering hazards; this design treats those hazards as constitutional constraints rather than optional mitigations.

Cryptographic verification authenticates the lease bytes and signer threshold. It does not prove that a signer was legally authorized, that a key was uncompromised, or that the target owner consented. Those remain identity, HSM/KMS, contracting, and operational gates.
