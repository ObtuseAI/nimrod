# nimrod master plan

Version: 0.1-draft  
Planning horizon: validation through general availability  
Status: `CONSTRUCTION_ZONE_PROVISIONING_SIGNED_DENIAL_INDEPENDENT_ATTESTATION_BLOCKED`

## Executive decision

nimrod should be built as a platform with a deliberately narrow first product. Attempting endpoint defense, network control, scam prevention, autonomous repair, enterprise federation, and critical-infrastructure support in one launch would be unsafe, untestable, and commercially incoherent.

The product is one constitutional platform with two separately gated surfaces:

- **nimrod Edge:** everyday local protection and verified recovery for people and devices.
- **nimrod Crucible:** authorized continuous security validation for enterprise environments, AI systems, and production-class digital twins.

Authorized offensive testing is a required Crucible capability. It is introduced through a no-execution simulator, disposable isolated ranges, isolated C2 ranges, and only then explicitly authorized production campaigns. No stage grants ambient authority: every action is constrained by a signed lease, immutable target graph, effect ceiling, budget, independent kill switch, cleanup contract, and evidence receipt.

The source repository remains private and proprietary in the `obtuseai` GitHub organization. Trademark clearance is paused indefinitely, so `nimrod` is an internal identifier and is not an approved externally marketed product name.

The accepted vNext architecture is the **Constitutional Autonomous Cyber Immune System (CACIS)** in `VNEXT_CACIS_MASTER_PLAN.md`. CACIS adds a versioned Cyber World Model, ephemeral offense/defense immune organisms, competing hypotheses, adversarial challenge, metacognition, homeostasis, Chronos, governed memory/genomes, security arenas, and an Observatory. It changes no authority: the Governor schedules but never authorizes; organisms propose but never execute; verification remains independent; evolution remains candidate-only; and all operational offense remains behind existing Crucible gates. ADR-069 records the selection.

The deployment-assurance edge retains four bounded proofs. The Crucible edge separates connector declaration, exact scope compilation, observation admission, independent acceptance, threshold-authorized evidence completion, public source intake, owner-scope exclusion, and source staging. Five public source candidates remain metadata-only. A 2-of-3 governance decision now binds the incomplete owner registry, source registry, replica plan, offline network, and eight quarantine requirements, then explicitly denies staging. Public hosts are never targets; owner and unknown ownership are denied. Source acquisition, quarantine, replica construction, range connection, and execution remain blocked alongside all real observations and acceptances, five of seven isolation controls, hardware-key custody, physical power-loss durability, connector operation, and production promotion.

The recommended first public launch wedge remains:

> **nimrod Edge Preview:** a Windows-first, local-first application for security-conscious individuals and small technical teams that observes a bounded set of endpoint and egress activity, produces typed evidence, explains risk, applies deterministic policy, offers reversible containment, and verifies recovery.

This wedge proves the hardest shared platform properties—identity, evidence, policy, bounded action, witness, and verification—without pretending the entire universal fabric exists.

## Outcome hierarchy

### North-star outcome

Users complete risky digital tasks with materially less harm and can verify what nimrod observed, decided, changed, and restored.

### Product outcomes

1. Detect selected high-value behaviors with measured precision and recall.
2. Explain every intervention in plain language with evidence and uncertainty.
3. Contain only within a defined, reversible action set.
4. Recover supported states and independently verify the result.
5. Keep sensitive telemetry local by default.
6. Preserve useful protection in offline and cloud-degraded states.

### Business outcomes

1. Establish a trusted design-partner cohort before a broad consumer launch.
2. Prove willingness to install, retain, and pay without surveillance monetization.
3. Build credibility through versioned auditable specifications, independent evaluation, and transparent product limitations without requiring public source code.
4. Reach repeatable support and update operations before adding product authority.

## Initial market and user

### Recommended first customer

Security-conscious Windows users and small technical teams with valuable accounts, source code, or business data, but without a full SOC.

### Primary jobs

- Tell me when a program, browser extension, or script is behaving outside its expected authority.
- Show what evidence supports the warning and what remains unknown.
- Let me safely stop or isolate the activity without losing my work.
- Verify that the threat is no longer active and that recovery succeeded.
- Keep my telemetry and investigation material under my control.

### Explicit non-targets for the initial release

- unattended enterprise-wide remediation;
- mobile-device protection;
- banking or payment blocking;
- child or elder monitoring;
- life-safety or critical-infrastructure control;
- air-gapped classified environments;
- generalized autonomous vulnerability patching;
- claims of replacing EDR, SIEM, backup, identity, or incident-response products.

## Delivery stages

Durations are planning ranges, not commitments. Every stage ends at an evidence gate; calendar pressure cannot waive a gate.

### Stage 0 — Private foundation and versioned specifications (6–10 weeks)

Objectives:

- validate the user, risk, and willingness-to-install assumptions;
- approve the doctrine, initial product boundary, and governance;
- enforce the owner-approved private-name boundary and complete IP, corporate, privacy, product-liability, and export-control review;
- define stable versioned schemas and conformance tests;
- establish secure development, signing, disclosure, and release operations;
- produce architecture prototypes that do not require privileged deployment.

Exit evidence:

- at least 20 structured discovery interviews across intended users and buyers;
- 5–8 design partners with written evaluation intent;
- verified private `obtuseai` repository governance, proprietary no-license posture, and external-identity release block;
- reviewed threat model and privacy data-flow map;
- accepted envelope, evidence, capability, plugin, and update specifications;
- two independent implementations pass schema conformance tests;
- CI identity, protected release process, SBOM, signing design, and key ceremony rehearsed;
- owner accepts the Stage 1 budget and residual-risk register.

### Stage 1 — Unprivileged technical proof (8–12 weeks)

Current evidence: the typed Python reference includes the replayed Edge Budget-1 proposal and proof chain plus a separate live read-only adapter that measures exactly one caller-selected Windows process through supported APIs and retains only hashed identity. Live process identity remains policy-input incomplete because destination, parent, publisher, and writable-path evidence are absent. The offline release foundation verifies threshold signatures, exact predecessor sequence, artifact bytes, provenance/SBOM presence, rollback declarations, and a deny-by-default WASM plugin manifest without installing or executing anything. The design-partner evidence plan defines five consent-first tasks and hard comprehension/privacy gates while preserving zero participants and zero contact. The Crucible reference retains its existing threshold authorization, Witness, verifier, proposal-swarm, and deny-first range gates. Real continuous sensing, containment, recovery, production custody, update rollout, plugin runtime enforcement, design-partner evidence, range evidence, and production behavior remain incomplete.

Build:

- local event simulator and replayable evidence bus;
- deterministic policy decision service;
- local content-addressed witness store;
- typed action compiler with a no-op executor;
- independent verifier process;
- explanation and proof-view prototype;
- fixture labeling that makes simulated evidence impossible to confuse with live evidence.

Exit evidence:

- complete trace from observation to policy, no-op action, verification, and receipt;
- prompt injection and hostile-content tests cannot create authority;
- schema migration, crash recovery, replay, tamper, and rollback tests pass;
- documented latency and storage envelope;
- no privileged installation and no production security claim.

### Stage 2 — Windows Edge Cell alpha (4–6 months)

Build:

- signed Windows service with the smallest viable privileged boundary;
- process creation and ancestry observation;
- executable provenance and selected file-change observation;
- DNS/connection metadata correlated to initiating process where supported;
- temporary process-tree suspension/termination and per-process egress restriction, each individually gated;
- local snapshots for supported settings;
- verified restoration for explicitly tested scenarios;
- protection, explanation, privacy, emergency, and proof views;
- offline update verification and safe uninstall.

Alpha constraints:

- invite-only; no general consumer marketing;
- observer mode by default;
- autonomy budget 0–1; budget 2 only for named reversible controls;
- no kernel driver unless a separately reviewed need cannot be met by supported platform APIs;
- no raw telemetry upload by default;
- every detector has an owner, version, calibration record, and disable path.

Exit evidence:

- supported Windows-version compatibility matrix;
- clean install, upgrade, rollback, uninstall, and emergency-disable validation;
- measured performance and false-positive budgets met on representative hardware;
- independent security assessment of privileged and update paths;
- design-partner harm and usability review;
- no open critical or high-severity release-blocking finding.

### Stage 3 — Private beta (3–4 months)

Objectives:

- test real-world calibration across diverse, consented environments;
- operate signed staged updates and emergency rollback;
- exercise vulnerability response, customer support, incident response, evidence export, and deletion;
- prove cloud outage does not disable essential local protections;
- test safe coexistence with major Windows security products.

Exit evidence:

- beta cohort and device-hour threshold approved in the evaluation plan;
- material false-positive, crash, and recovery-failure rates below published thresholds;
- response and update exercises meet objectives;
- third-party penetration test closed or risk-accepted by accountable owners;
- accessibility review demonstrates WCAG 2.2 AA for applicable UI plus keyboard and screen-reader workflows;
- legal launch review updated against actual data flows and product behavior.

### Stage 4 — Public preview (2–3 months)

Scope:

- opt-in download with explicit preview limitations;
- public documentation, threat model summary, security policy, signed artifacts, checksums, SBOM, release notes, known issues, privacy notice, support and EOL policy;
- public bug bounty or funded coordinated disclosure program;
- telemetry remains opt-in, minimized, and purpose-bound;
- no “complete protection” or replacement-product claim.

Exit evidence:

- public release gates in `PUBLIC_LAUNCH.md` satisfied;
- independent reproducibility or provenance verification;
- on-call, rollback, status communication, and incident notification rehearsed;
- observed reliability and support load can be sustained.

### Stage 5 — General availability (GA) (3–6 months after preview evidence)

GA is a support and assurance state, not a marketing date.

Required outcomes:

- stable supported-scenario contract and service-level objectives;
- defined paid or sustainable operating model;
- completed applicable regulatory conformity plan;
- mature update, vulnerability, privacy-rights, support, and end-of-life operations;
- independent assessment results and limitations published;
- product liability and cyber insurance reviewed;
- no unresolved launch blocker.

### Stage 6 — Crucible range alpha and production-safe beta (gated, 6–12 months)

Crucible begins as a no-execution simulator and isolated cyber-range product. It does not inherit Edge release authority.

Build:

- signed authorization leases and customer-controlled kill switches;
- Protection Profiles for Windows, Linux, cloud workloads, identity systems, and AI agents;
- a Counterfactual Coverage Twin;
- ATT&CK and ATLAS campaign mapping;
- D3FEND countermeasure mapping;
- OCSF telemetry normalization with raw evidence retained by digest;
- CACAO/OpenC2 import that compiles into nimrod action envelopes;
- Atomic Red Team and Caldera range connectors;
- Wazuh, Velociraptor, Elastic/OpenSearch, VECTR, and read-only BloodHound connectors;
- a causal attack-defense-repair graph and time-decaying assurance vectors;
- matched positive and negative controls;
- recursive improvement quarantine, replay, sealed evaluation, champion floor, canary, promotion, and demotion paths.

Production-safe beta may begin only after range evidence proves target containment, lease revocation, kill-switch independence, cleanup, recovery, cross-tenant isolation, and connector-compromise containment. Ordinary production uses the safe-realism ceiling: real authorized techniques are permitted, while destructive, exfiltration, firmware, and physical-safety effects remain simulated.

Mythic and Sliver connectors enter only after the dedicated C2 isolation gate passes. Commercial connectors for Cobalt Strike, Brute Ratel, Splunk, CrowdStrike, Scythe, and AttackIQ follow the same contract and licensing review. External tools remain customer-managed and replaceable.

### Stage 7 — Universal profile expansion (gated, multi-year)

Only after GA evidence:

1. Identity and egress mesh.
2. Browser/email ScamShield with independent contact verification.
3. Trustworthiness graph and threat cells.
4. Proof-carrying repair in development and staging environments.
5. Enterprise federation and SOC integrations.
6. Federated immune exchange.
7. Critical-infrastructure profile.

Each expansion is a separate product safety case. A prior release does not authorize a broader domain.

## Crucible outcome hierarchy

Crucible does not ask only whether an alert fired. It proves the causal chain:

```text
authorized intent
→ attempted technique
→ actual state delta
→ sensor observation
→ normalized telemetry
→ detection or prevention decision
→ response
→ recovery
→ independently verified post-state
```

Its Assurance Vector reports prevention, observation, detection, correlation, response, recovery, false interruption, evidence completeness, privacy exposure, performance cost, uncertainty, and evidence age. No single percentage may replace these dimensions.

## AI controllability and repair

Every supported AI system is wrapped by an AI Capsule that versions model identity, system policy, prompts, retrieval context, memory lineage, tool registry, data permissions, capability leases, budgets, and recovery state. Agent tool calls use action envelopes; model responses never directly authorize tools.

AI repair uses signed snapshots and independently verified restoration of prompts, policies, tools, memories, indexes, model versions, and runtime configuration. Crucible validates prompt injection, context poisoning, tool poisoning, memory corruption, credential harvesting, data leakage, runaway delegation, evaluator manipulation, unsafe self-replication, and rollback failure using ATT&CK/ATLAS-linked campaigns.

## Technical strategy

### CACIS vNext sequence

CACIS is delivered as eight additional evidence gates, not as a big-bang rewrite: constitutional spine; world-model replay; immune-organism lifecycle; hypothesis and metacognitive cortex; metabolism/homeostasis/Chronos; genome and partitioned evaluation; arenas and Observatory; then separately authorized Crucible integration. W1 now has an offline deterministic six-domain replay plus predecessor-bound successor generations, durable source cursors, explicit deduplication and gap evidence, threshold-signed replay source policy/health/intake decisions, bounded no-drop backpressure, retention projection enforcement, separate-process causal verification, and recovery of the world-ahead/cursor-prepared crash window. W2 includes credential-theft and suspicious-script morphologies with Shadow teardown. W3 binds both lifecycles and uses a distinct read-only verifier process. W4 adds bounded metabolism, homeostasis, and Chronos. W5 adds nine memory strata, three replay partitions, nine reward-hacking defenses, seven complexity metrics, and candidate-only distillation. W6 adds fifteen arenas, fourteen benchmark dimensions, and a two-role threshold-signed display-only Observatory projection. W7 remains blocked by existing live range gates; none of W1-W6 grants runtime protection or execution authority.

### Build principles

- Rust for new privileged and security-critical native components unless a platform boundary requires another language.
- Stable Protobuf/CBOR-compatible contracts with canonical JSON representations for inspection.
- WASM component/plugin boundary with signed manifests and deny-by-default capabilities.
- Deterministic policy evaluation separated from analysis.
- Local append-only evidence journal backed by content-addressed artifacts.
- Explicit connector interfaces for platform APIs, identity providers, storage, model gateways, and external tools.
- Replaceable analytics and models; no vendor-specific data model in public contracts.
- Reproducible, signed, anti-rollback releases with staged rollout.

### Buy, integrate, or build

Build the differentiated trust boundaries:

- action/evidence envelope;
- authority kernel and capability semantics;
- epistemic firewall;
- evidence witness and verification protocol;
- autonomy budget and harm circuit breaker;
- user proof experience.

Integrate mature foundations where contracts permit replacement:

- endpoint query and detection tools;
- policy engines;
- workload identity;
- observability;
- artifact signing and transparency;
- vulnerability databases and rule formats;
- durable workflow and storage.

Do not fork a large security platform merely to rename it nimrod.

## Metrics and thresholds

Exact numeric thresholds are approved during Stage 0 after baselines exist. They must include:

| Category | Metric |
|---|---|
| Security efficacy | scenario prevention/detection, containment time, verified recovery rate |
| Safety | unauthorized-action count, rollback failure, destructive side effect, evidence integrity failure |
| Calibration | precision, recall, confidence calibration, false interruption per device-day |
| Reliability | crash-free device-hours, update success, rollback success, offline continuity |
| Performance | CPU, memory, disk, battery, boot/login, and network overhead |
| Privacy | bytes/fields collected, local-vs-exported ratio, deletion completion, unauthorized access |
| Usability | safe task completion, explanation comprehension, override safety, accessibility |
| Operations | acknowledge/contain/remediate times, support burden, release rollback time |
| Business | install completion, retained protected devices, paid conversion, support-adjusted margin |

Hard failures always override averages: unauthorized consequential action, fabricated evidence, unreported destructive effect, secret disclosure, false success, unsafe update, or hidden policy weakening.

## Funding gates

Fund one stage at a time. Each investment memo contains scope, evidence, unresolved risks, next-stage cost range, and stop criteria. Do not raise or spend against claims that the universal roadmap is already implemented.

## Stop or pivot criteria

Pause or narrow the product if any of these persist after a defined remediation window:

- users will not grant the installation privileges needed for the promised value;
- false interruptions or performance cost cannot meet the approved budget;
- recovery cannot be verified reliably;
- platform APIs cannot support the required control without an unsafe privileged footprint;
- privacy-preserving local operation is commercially unsustainable;
- name or IP risk cannot be cleared;
- the team cannot sustain separation of duties and secure release operations;
- measured value is not meaningfully better than a simpler focused tool.

## Definition of preparation complete

Preparation is complete when the owner can make the Stage 0 decisions without inventing scope, safety doctrine, release gates, architecture boundaries, legal work, success metrics, or first backlog. This packet meets that definition; implementation authorization remains a separate decision.
